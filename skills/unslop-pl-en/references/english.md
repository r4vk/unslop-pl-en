# English: word tiers, patterns, and issues for Polish authors

Load for every English check, rewrite, or translation into English. All examples are invented.

Hits are candidates. Technical, quoted, attributed, literal and brand uses stay. A listed word carrying real content is not a finding.

## 1. Word tiers

### Tier 1: inspect every hit, replace stock uses

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| delve, dive into, deep dive | look at, go through, get to the point |
| leverage (verb), utilize | use |
| foster, facilitate, empower | help, allow, give X the tools to |
| streamline, supercharge, harness | simplify, speed up (say by how much) |
| robust, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, best-in-class | name the property: handles 10k requests/s, passed audit X |
| seamless, frictionless | say which step disappears |
| holistic, end-to-end (as buzzword), turnkey (loose) | list what is covered |
| game changer, paradigm shift, revolutionary, transformative, groundbreaking | describe what changes, for whom, by how much |
| unlock (potential, value), unleash | say what becomes possible |
| take it to the next level, elevate | say what improves |
| tapestry, landscape (loose), realm, beacon, cornerstone, backbone, linchpin | the plain noun for what it is |
| multifaceted, intricate, meticulous, paramount, pivotal | list the facets, or cut |
| journey (digital, customer; loose) | process, path |
| ever-evolving, fast-paced, in today's world | cut or name the change |
| stands as a testament, marks a pivotal moment, plays a vital role | state the fact |
| tailored, bespoke (filler) | tailored to what (name it) |

### Tier 2: flag when two or more cluster

comprehensive, innovative, scalable (without numbers), optimize, impactful, crucial, essential, vital, significant (outside statistics), notable, remarkable, vibrant, dynamic, strategic, actionable, insights, ecosystem (loose), stakeholders (loose), solutions (as a noun for everything), synergy, value-add

### Tier 3: flag only when they pile up

important, key, effective, efficient, powerful, unique, modern, professional, high-quality, various, numerous, wide range, overall, ultimately

### Often-empty adverbs and phrases

Adverbs: just, literally, honestly, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally, importantly, crucially, inherently, inevitably. Cut when they add nothing; keep when they carry emphasis or real uncertainty.

Phrases: it's worth noting, it's important to note, at the end of the day, when it comes to, at its core, in the world of, in the age of, the reality is, in terms of, going forward, in order to (usually "to"), let's dive in, here's the thing, let me be clear, I'll be honest.

## 2. Patterns

- **Negative parallelism:** "It's not X, it's Y", "Not X. Not Y. Just Z.", "X isn't a Y, it's a Z", "less X, more Y", the tail ", no guessing". Allowed for corrections ("Version 3, not 2."), corrected figures, instructions and authenticity. Flag at three or more per document. The invented-opponent form ("a builder, not a hype merchant") is flagged even once: replace with what the person does, from facts in the text.
- **Faux-insight setup:** "What most people miss", "Here's what nobody tells you", "The part everyone skips". Cut the setup; let the claim stand.
- **Colon reveal:** "The detail that makes it work: a second review pass." -> a plain sentence. Capitalize after a colon only for grammar, names, titles or code.
- **Superficial -ing tail:** ", highlighting the team's commitment", ", underscoring…", ", reflecting…", ", showcasing…". Cut or replace with a fact.
- **Importance puffery and metadiscourse:** "This distinction matters", "That's more important than it sounds", "As you can see", "In other words" after a clear sentence. Delete; where the point is not clear, the missing fact goes in its place.
- **Weasel attribution:** "Experts agree", "studies show", "many argue", "widely regarded". Name the source or cut.
- **Fake-strong verbs:** "serves as", "boasts", "features", "offers" for "is", "has". Use the plain verb when the meaning is unchanged.
- **Synonym cycling:** repeat the right word instead of rotating "tool / platform / solution / system".
- **Triads and tricolons:** "fast, simple and secure". Use two or four, or a sentence. Keep three where the meaning has three parts.
- **Slogan cadence:** "Click less. Ship more.", "One login. Five systems." Three per document is a template.
- **Rhetorical setups:** "What if I told you…", "Think about it:", "Here's the kicker".
- **Staged emphasis:** "every. single. day.", "Read that again.", "Let that sink in."
- **Kickers and summary endings:** "The tools change. The craft doesn't." / "In conclusion…". Delete; end on the most concrete sentence.
- **Realization coda** in stories: "…and that's when I realized". End on the event.
- **Formatting:** emoji in headings, bold in mid-sentence, bullets that chop one argument, a heading over two sentences.
- **Punctuation budget:** em dash at most about one per 500 words, none on slides; exclamation mark about one per 1,000 words; use colons and semicolons.

## 3. Typography

Straight or curly quotes: pick one and keep it. Oxford comma: pick one. Headings: sentence case or title case, consistently (consistent title case is not a tell in English). Spell out numbers under ten in running prose unless the source style differs; keep digits for measurements and dates.

## 4. Issues common to Polish authors (quality, not AI tells)

Check these when the English text was written or translated from Polish. They are fixed on sight, not scored as slop.

| Pattern from Polish | English |
|---|---|
| aktualnie -> "actually" | currently (actually = in fact) |
| ewentualnie -> "eventually" | possibly, optionally (eventually = in the end) |
| realizować -> "realize" | carry out, implement (realize = understand) |
| kontrolować -> "control" | check, verify (control = have power over) |
| ewidencja -> "evidence" | records, register (evidence = proof) |
| dedykowany -> "dedicated" for "przeznaczony dla" | built for, intended for; "dedicated" means committed or exclusive |
| konsekwentnie -> "consequently" | consistently (consequently = as a result) |
| w sprawie -> "in the matter of" | regarding, about |
| "It is worth to…" | It is worth + -ing: "It is worth checking" |
| "according to me" | in my view, I think |
| "the same" as filler for "it" | it, this |
| missing or extra articles | check a/an/the against what is known to the reader |
| long chains of participles and "which" | split into two sentences with an actor and a verb |
| nominal stacks from Polish "wdrożenie rozwiązania do zarządzania…" | "implementing the management tool" |

## 5. Look-alikes to leave alone

- "Version 3, not 2." / "The drop was 40%, not 4%."
- "The photo is real, not a render."
- "Robust" in structural engineering or statistics; "ecosystem" around a platform with named parts; "landscape" in geography.
- Calibrated hedging in scientific or legal text.
- One em dash used for a real interruption in a long text.
